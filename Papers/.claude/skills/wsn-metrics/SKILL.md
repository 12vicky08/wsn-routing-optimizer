# WSN Performance Metrics — Definitions and Formulas

Every metric used in the paper must be defined rigorously in Section 5 (System Model) or at first use in Section 8 (Results). Use these definitions verbatim as the technical basis; do not invent alternate formulas.

## 1. Throughput
**Definition:** Rate of successfully delivered data at the sink/base station.
**Unit:** kbps or Mbps (bits/sec), sometimes packets/sec.
**Formula:** `Throughput = (Total bits successfully received at sink) / (Total simulation time)`
**Plotting convention:** x-axis = simulation time or round number; y-axis = throughput. Line plot, one line per compared scheme.

## 2. Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR)
**Definition:** Fraction of packets generated by source nodes that are successfully received at the sink.
**Unit:** % (dimensionless ratio × 100)
**Formula:** `PDR = (Packets received at sink / Packets generated by sources) × 100%`
**Note:** distinct from raw "packets delivered" (a cumulative count, not a ratio). If our figures show cumulative delivered packets rather than PDR%, that is throughput/delivery-count data, NOT PDR — do not relabel it as PDR without recomputing against packets generated.
**Plotting convention:** x-axis = round/time or traffic load; y-axis = PDR (%), typically 0-100 scale.

## 3. Network Lifetime
**Definition:** Duration the network remains operationally useful, measured via node-death milestones:
- **FND (First Node Dies):** round/time at which the first sensor node exhausts its energy.
- **HND (Half Node Dies):** round/time at which 50% of nodes have died.
- **LND (Last Node Dies):** round/time at which all nodes have died (network fully non-functional).
**Unit:** rounds (simulation rounds) or seconds, depending on simulator convention.
**Plotting convention:** bar chart comparing FND/HND/LND across schemes, or a "alive nodes vs. round" survival curve.

## 4. End-to-End Delay
**Definition:** Time elapsed from packet generation at a source node to successful reception at the sink, averaged over all delivered packets.
**Unit:** ms (milliseconds) or seconds.
**Formula:** `E2E Delay = (1/N) × Σ(T_received_i − T_generated_i)` for i = 1..N delivered packets.
**Plotting convention:** x-axis = traffic load or node count; y-axis = average delay (ms).

## 5. Energy Consumption (Residual / Consumed)
**Definition:** Total or per-node energy expended on transmission, reception, sensing, and idle listening. May be reported as cumulative consumed energy or residual (remaining) energy.
**Unit:** Joules (J).
**Formula (first-order radio model, most common in WSN literature):**
- Transmit: `E_TX(k, d) = E_elec × k + ε_amp × k × d^n` (n=2 free-space or n=4 multipath, threshold-dependent)
- Receive: `E_RX(k) = E_elec × k`
- where k = packet size (bits), d = distance (m), E_elec = electronics energy/bit, ε_amp = amplifier energy coefficient.
**Plotting convention:** x-axis = round/time; y-axis = cumulative energy consumed (J) or residual energy (J), one line per scheme.

## 6. Traffic (Offered Load / Traffic-Load Sensitivity)
**Definition:** The rate at which source nodes generate/offer traffic into the network, used as an independent variable to test how other metrics (throughput, PDR, delay) degrade under increasing load.
**Unit:** packets/sec (pkt/s) or kbps.
**Plotting convention:** x-axis = offered load (pkt/s or kbps); y-axis = the dependent metric being stress-tested (throughput, PDR, or delay) — this is a sensitivity sweep, not a time-series.

## General rules for this paper
- Every figure caption must state: metric, unit, x-axis variable, and compared schemes.
- Never relabel a cumulative-count plot as a ratio/percentage metric or vice versa — check axis units before writing prose about a figure.
- If a required metric has no corresponding figure or raw data, it must be marked as a gap in analysis/data-gaps.md — do not write results prose for a metric without a figure.
